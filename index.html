Virginia Law Update · Effective July 1, 2026

Virginia's Notary Laws
Changed.
Are You Compliant?

New state law now requires every Virginia notary — paper and electronic — to keep a recorded journal of each notarial act. If you're a notary, settlement agent, paralegal, or real estate professional in Virginia, this applies to you. Today.

What Changed

These requirements took effect July 1, 2026. If you haven't started yet, start now.

📓

Journal Required for All Notarial Acts

Every notarial act — acknowledgments, jurats, copy certifications, signature witnessings — must now be logged in a recorded journal. Previously, only electronic notaries had to keep one. Now it applies to all notaries.

Va. Code § 47.1-16

📅

5-Year Retention Requirement

All journal records — paper or electronic — must be retained for a minimum of five years from the date of each transaction. That means every entry from July 1, 2026 onward.

🔏

Proof of Commission Required to Order Your Seal

When purchasing or renewing your notary seal or stamp, you must present proof of your commission to the vendor. Both you and the vendor are required to retain that documentation for five years.

HB 163 / SB 316

🏠

Settlement Agent Standard of Care

There is now a statutory duty for settlement agents to take reasonable steps to verify identity and ensure proper handling of real estate transactions. Expect more documentation requirements at closings.
